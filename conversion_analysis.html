Product-Market Fit Analysis · VideoText.io

Does VideoText have product-market fit?

A ground-up read of 70,888 PostHog events across 4,995 tracked persons, Feb 11 – Jul 26 2026 (~5.5 months). Every number below is queried directly from PostHog — nothing is assumed. Where the data is thin, contaminated by test accounts, or simply not instrumented, that is flagged next to the number, not buried in a footnote.

Executive Summary

3

Early signal, not yet product-market fit.

A handful of real people found enough value to pay and renew — but 91.5% of everyone who ever ran a job ran exactly one, daily engagement is flat at ~4% of monthly users, and confirmed recurring revenue sits at an estimated 1–2 active paying customers after 5.5 months. Growth in raw traffic is real and accelerating; retained, paying usage is not.

Confidence: Low

Confidence is capped at low deliberately: the entire paid-usage picture rests on roughly a dozen real (non-founder, non-demo) individuals, and there is no dollar-amount data anywhere in PostHog to verify revenue. Directional conclusions are solid; precise numbers are not.

Top 3 action items

  1. Instrument revenue properly — add an amount/currency field or connect Stripe as a PostHog data source. Right now nobody, including this report, can state VideoText's actual MRR.
  2. Fix the payment_completed event — it fires 0–12 seconds after checkout_started for nearly every user, meaning it tracks checkout initiation, not confirmed payment. This event is currently unsafe to use as a revenue signal anywhere in the business.
  3. Chase the one-and-done problem — 91.5% of job-creators never come back. Before spending on acquisition, find out why the other 8.5% did a second job and try to replicate it (video-to-transcript and fix-subtitles show the best, if still weak, repeat signal).

6. Revenue

PostHog has no price data. No event anywhere (payment_completed, plan_upgraded, plan_clicked, checkout_started) carries an amount or currency property. Only 1 person in the entire project has a non-null stripe_customer_id. No Stripe (or any) data warehouse source is connected (system.information_schema.tables WHERE table_type='data_warehouse' is empty). MRR, ARR, and ARPU cannot be computed from this data — full stop. Anything about revenue below is customer counts and behavior only, never dollars.

A critical event-naming problem

payment_completed fires 0–12 seconds after checkout_started for nearly every matched pair (checked across all 26 events). That is the timing signature of a checkout redirect, not a confirmed Stripe payment — real card processing and webhook confirmation take longer and happen asynchronously. Every payment_completed event also carries type: "subscription_checkout_started", which corroborates this directly. This event should not be trusted as proof of actual payment anywhere in the business until it's re-wired to fire from a Stripe webhook (see Section 10).

This is far too small a sample to trust as a real churn rate, but directionally it lines up with the rest of the report: the product converts a few people to paid, and struggles to keep them.

9. PMF Assessment

3
/ 10
Not yet PMF — early, narrow signal only.

Confidence: low. The entire "product-market fit" case rests on fewer than 15 identifiable real people who ever paid, ~5 who renewed, and an 8.5% second-job rate on a base of 967 real users. That's enough to say "something real is happening for a few people," not enough to say "this works."

Strongest evidence for PMF

  • Real renewals exist. At least 4 identifiable, non-founder people (umair624, erikdixon224, mounir.benammour1, hannah.simmons@edtechhub.org) completed a full paid billing cycle and renewed — someone found it worth paying for twice.
  • 100% organic acquisition across 30 countries with no paid channel — the product is being found and signed up for without any spend, and signups are accelerating (Apr 2 → May 23 → Jun 25 → Jul 38, and still climbing).
  • A coherent core-product story: video-to-transcript is simultaneously the most-used tool (56% of jobs), the best repeat-usage driver among high-volume tools (9.3%), and the only tool that has ever led to a paid checkout. The product's central hypothesis is where all the signal concentrates.
  • WAU is climbing in the most recent weeks (132 → 126 → 185 over the last 3 weeks of data) — the most recent trend, not just the historical average, is positive.

Biggest weaknesses

  • 91.5% one-and-done. Of 967 real users who ever created a job, 885 never came back for a second one.
  • Stickiness is flat and low at ~3.6–4.9% DAU/MAU every month, regardless of how much MAU grew — growth is bringing in volume, not habit.
  • New:returning ratio of ~81:1 in monthly lifecycle — almost the entire active base every month is brand new, never people coming back.
  • Vanishing monetization funnel. In the strict, time-ordered funnel, only 1 of 1,077 visitors ever reached checkout, and 0 reached renewal within that same sequential path.
  • Revenue is fundamentally unverifiable. No dollar amounts anywhere in PostHog; the closest thing to a "payment" event fires on checkout initiation, not confirmation.

Top 5 recommendations, ranked by expected PMF impact

  1. 1
    Instrument real revenue data
    Add amount, currency, and billing interval to payment/subscription events, or connect Stripe as a PostHog Data Pipeline source. Without this, nobody at VideoText — including this report — can state actual MRR, ARR, or LTV. This is the single highest-leverage fix: it doesn't change the product, it just makes the truth visible.
  2. 2
    Re-wire payment_completed to fire on Stripe webhook confirmation
    Currently it fires 0–12 seconds after checkout starts — that's a redirect, not a payment. Trigger it from invoice.paid / checkout.session.completed server-side so every downstream funnel and revenue number stops being contaminated by abandoned checkouts.
  3. 3
    Understand the 8.5% who came back for a 2nd job
    Interview or event-trace the 82 real repeat users, especially the fix-subtitles and video-to-transcript repeaters. Find what triggered the return visit and try to manufacture that trigger (email, in-app nudge, credit) for the other 91.5%.
  4. 4
    Close the signup-tracking gap
    974 people created jobs but only 88 ever fire signup_completed — either there's an untracked guest flow or the event is missing on a large class of real signups. Fix this before trusting any funnel or retention number built on top of it.
  5. 5
    Fix server-side event context
    88% of job_created events carry no browser or device data, meaning most real usage is invisible to segmentation. Attach person/device context to the server-side job pipeline so future country/device/browser analysis reflects the real user base, not the 12% with client context.

10. Missing Instrumentation — the exact gaps

Every place in this report where a number is marked GAP or ESTIMATE traces back to one of these six root causes.

No revenue amounts anywhere

Checked every payment-adjacent event (payment_completed, plan_upgraded, plan_clicked, checkout_started, subscription_renewed) for a price field — none exists. Only plan (a tier name, not a price) is present. Fix: add amount (integer, cents) and currency (ISO code) to payment_completed and subscription_renewed, or connect Stripe via PostHog's Data Warehouse feature.

payment_completed fires at the wrong moment

Fires 0–12 seconds after checkout_started for nearly all users and always carries type: "subscription_checkout_started" — this is a checkout-initiation event mislabeled as a payment-completion event. Fix: rename the existing event to checkout_redirect_initiated and add a genuine payment_completed triggered server-side from the Stripe webhook.

88% of job_created events have no browser/device/person context

854 of 974 job-creators show $browser and $device_type as null — this event appears to fire from a backend job queue without forwarding client context. Fix: pass $browser, $device_type, and $os (or the originating session id) through to the server-side event, or fire a client-side companion event at job submission time.

signup_completed covers a fraction of real users

88 tracked signups vs. 974 people who created a job — a 10x gap. Either a guest/anonymous flow lets people use the product without a tracked signup, or the event was added late and back-fills nothing. Fix: confirm whether a guest flow exists; if so, add an explicit guest_session_started event so guest vs. registered usage can be told apart. If not, audit why signup_completed under-fires.

No way to tell current subscription status

There's no subscription_status or is_active person property, so "currently paying" must be inferred from the last-seen event per person (renewed vs. deleted), which is fragile at this scale. Fix: set a person.properties.subscription_status (active/canceled/past_due) on every billing webhook so current paying-customer count is a single property filter, not an inference.

Stripe customer ID barely populated

Only 1 person across the entire project has stripe_customer_id set, despite 18+ people reaching checkout. Fix: set this person property immediately after Stripe customer creation, not only in edge cases — it's the join key that would let PostHog ever connect to real Stripe revenue data.
