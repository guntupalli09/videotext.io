# How much does subtitle translation cost per minute?

Subtitle translation costs $0.50 to $30 per minute of video in 2026, and the number swings that wide because "translation" means three different jobs: raw machine output, machine translation with a human pass, and full human translation from scratch. The rate you quote a client — or get quoted by a vendor — depends on which of those three you're buying, plus turnaround and language pair. Most per-minute quotes leave out one cost entirely: resyncing timing after the translated text no longer fits the original cue length.

## How much does subtitle translation cost per minute?

The rate depends on tier, turnaround, and language pair. Here's the breakdown for 2026:

| Machine translation only | $0.50-$2 | Minutes | Internal review drafts |

A 30-minute episode translated at the MTPE rate lands between $90 and $240. The same episode at full human translation with a rush turnaround can hit $600 to $900. Neither figure includes the time it takes to re-check character-per-line (CPL) limits once the translated text is longer or shorter than the source.

### Machine translation only: $0.50 to $2 per minute

This tier is raw MT engine output dropped into the subtitle file — no proofreading, no CPL check, no reading-speed check. It's fast and cheap, and it is not client-ready. Use it for internal review, rough drafts, or to gauge whether a video is even worth translating fully. Deliver it as final work and expect rejected files.

### MT + human post-edit (MTPE): $3 to $8 per minute

A human translator reviews and corrects the machine output line by line instead of translating from scratch. This cuts turnaround roughly in half compared to full human translation while catching the errors MT alone misses — wrong idioms, dropped negatives, mistranslated proper nouns. Most standard client subtitle jobs in 2026 land here.

### Full human translation: $8 to $20 per minute

A translator works from the source subtitle file line by line, choosing phrasing that fits the target language's CPL and reading-speed limits rather than just translating the meaning. This is the tier for legal depositions, broadcast content, and anything with contractual accuracy requirements. It's also the tier where language pair moves the price the most — English to Spanish sits at the low end, English to Japanese or Korean sits at the high end because fewer qualified subtitle translators work those pairs at volume.

### Rush turnaround: $20 to $30 per minute

Same full-human quality, compressed into 24-48 hours instead of 3-5 days. The premium exists because rush jobs often get split across two or three translators to hit the deadline, and someone has to reconcile terminology and tone across the split before delivery.

## Why subtitle translation costs vary

- **Language pair** — common pairs (English-Spanish, English-French) have more available translators and lower per-minute rates than rare pairs (English-Finnish, English-Korean).
- **Turnaround time** — rush jobs under 48 hours carry a premium over standard 3-5 day delivery.
- **Text expansion** — German and Finnish translations typically run 20-35% longer than the English source, which forces line-break and CPL rework the original quote didn't account for.
- **Subtitle format** — a plain SRT/VTT translation costs less than one that also needs burning into the video, since burn-in requires a re-render pass.
- **Technical vocabulary** — legal, medical, and engineering content needs subject-matter translators, which narrows the pool and raises the rate.
- **File length** — per-minute rates often drop on longer projects; a 60-minute file frequently prices lower per minute than a 5-minute clip because setup time gets spread across more minutes.

## Is machine-translated subtitles good enough for client delivery?

No — machine translation alone, at $0.50 to $2 per minute, skips the CPL and reading-speed checks that client style guides require, and it regularly mistranslates idioms and proper nouns. It works as an internal draft, not a final deliverable, unless a human reviewer passes over every cue first.

## Does subtitle translation preserve timing sync?

Only if the workflow keeps the original cue timestamps and checks the new text length against reading-speed limits for the target language. Translated text that runs longer than the source — which is common with German and Finnish — needs the cue re-split or the timing shifted, or the subtitle will scroll off-screen before a viewer finishes reading it.

What's the difference between machine translation and human subtitle translation?

Machine translation converts text automatically with no review, while human translation involves a translator checking phrasing, idioms, and CPL fit line by line. The gap in price ($0.50-$2 vs $8-$20 per minute) reflects that review time.

## One last thing

Text expansion is the line item most freelancers forget to quote for. A German or Finnish translation running 20-35% longer than the English source means cues that fit at 42 characters per line in English blow past CPL limits in the target language, and every one of those lines needs re-splitting before delivery. Build a CPL re-check pass into every translation quote, even the MTPE tier, or that rework eats the margin you priced in.
