## Selected primary angle

**Transcription statistics** at `/transcription-statistics/`.

Working title pattern:

> Transcription Statistics: {N} Facts, Trends & Benchmarks for 2026

`{N}` is the count of **retained, validated** statistics — never a padded “100+”.

### Why this angle

1. **Citation demand.** Writers covering accessibility law, creator workflows, education, podcasts, and AI products all need numbers about *transcription and the conditions that make transcripts/captions necessary*. One umbrella page is easier to cite than eight thin spin-offs.
2. **Primary sources exist.** WHO, NIDCD, CDC/NCHS, FCC, Ofcom, Pew, Edison Research, NCES, WebAIM, Mozilla Common Voice, the OpenAI Whisper paper, OpenSLR/LibriSpeech, and 3Play’s disclosed 2024 survey are all locatable originals.
3. **VideoText relevance.** The product converts video/audio to transcripts and SRT/VTT. The hub can point at real workflows without pretending VideoText originated the statistics.
4. **SERP opportunity.** Competitor “statistics” pages lean on unverifiable market-size CAGRs. A source-traced, reject-heavy page is materially easier to trust.
5. **Internal links.** Natural connections to `/video-to-transcript`, `/video-to-subtitles`, `/video-to-srt`, `/translate-subtitles`, `/fix-subtitles`, `/glossary/*`, `/open`, and `/research/transcription-accuracy-benchmark-2026`.

### Why not eight pages

Splitting into caption / podcast / accuracy / AI pages would:

- dilute one exceptional resource
- collide with existing research and commercial URLs
- create thin near-duplicates

Narrower angles become **sections**, not URLs.
